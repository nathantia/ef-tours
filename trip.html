Day 1 · London

Arrive, settle in, and read the city.

Arrival day

Meet your Tour Director, get oriented, and take your first walk through the city. The goal is not to memorize London—it’s to begin noticing how it works.

Navigate your first neighborhood, learn the group rhythm, and practice simple travel routines with support close by.

You’re practicingAdaptabilityConfidence

What feels different first—pace, sound, transit, architecture, or how people use public space?

Capture one photo of something ordinary that immediately tells you you’re somewhere new. Use it later as the opening image in a trip reflection.

Day 2 · London

Westminster, Parliament & the Thames.

Civics + history

Walk through the heart of London’s civic life and see how government, ceremony, history, and everyday movement share the same streets.

Walk Westminster, see Parliament up close, cross the Thames, and navigate part of central London as a group.

You’re practicingCultural awarenessCuriosity

How do buildings, monuments, guards, and public spaces communicate power? Who are these spaces designed to impress, include, or represent?

Photograph one public space that says something about British identity. Compare it later with a civic space in your own community.

Class connection · History · Civics · Design
Day 3 · London

Stories, neighborhoods & everyday culture.

Culture + independence

Move beyond the major landmarks and pay attention to how neighborhoods, markets, transit, and daily routines shape the city.

Explore a neighborhood, make small choices with your group, and practice navigating an unfamiliar place with growing independence.

You’re practicingAgencyCommunication

What do you learn from the moments between landmarks—signs, queues, transit, food, and how people share space?

Write three observations that no landmark description could have told you before arriving.

Class connection · Writing · Culture
Day 4 · Paris

Cross the Channel and reset your assumptions.

Travel + comparison

Travel to Paris and compare the feeling of arriving in a second major city. Notice what changes immediately and what feels unexpectedly familiar.

Travel to Paris, orient yourself to a new city, and begin comparing how two capitals communicate identity.

You’re practicingOpen-mindednessAdaptability

Which assumptions from London still work here—and which ones stop working immediately?

Create a quick two-column comparison: London / Paris. Add one observation each about movement, architecture, and public life.

Class connection · Geography · Culture
Day 5 · Paris

The Louvre: when scale changes interpretation.

Art + perspective

See major works in context and notice what changes when art is no longer a small image on a screen or page.

Move through the Louvre, encounter work at full scale, and compare a familiar image with the physical object in front of you.

You’re practicingCuriositySelf-awareness

Choose one artwork that feels different in person. Is it scale, texture, placement, crowd behavior, or something else?

Explain how the physical setting changed your interpretation of one artwork or object.

Class connection · Art · History · Writing
Day 6 · Paris

Culture lives outside the museum too.

Daily life + language

Explore everyday Paris through neighborhood streets, food, language, and small interactions that make culture feel lived rather than displayed.

Practice a simple interaction, try something unfamiliar, and pay attention to how people use streets, cafés, and public spaces.

You’re practicingCommunicationConfidence

What does everyday behavior teach you about culture that a famous landmark cannot?

Record one phrase, routine, or social cue you noticed and explain what it revealed about daily life.

Class connection · Language · Culture
Day 7 · Rome

Arrive where the ancient city is still visible.

History in context

Arrive in Rome and begin noticing how layers of history coexist with contemporary streets, traffic, businesses, and neighborhoods.

Orient yourself to Rome and see how ancient structures remain part of the visual and practical life of the city.

You’re practicingCultural awarenessPerspective

Where do you see the city preserving the past, adapting it, or simply living around it?

Photograph one place where two time periods visibly overlap.

Class connection · History · Urban studies
Day 8 · Rome

The Colosseum & Forum: read power from the ground up.

Archaeology + civics

Stand inside the spaces where public life, spectacle, religion, politics, and engineering came together.

Walk the Colosseum and Forum, use the physical layout as evidence, and connect ruins to the social systems they once supported.

You’re practicingCritical observationCuriosity

What was this place built to make people think, feel, or do? Who had access, and who held power?

Build an argument about Roman public life using three pieces of visual evidence you collected on-site.

Class connection · History · Civics
Day 9 · Rome

Connect belief, art, food & everyday life.

Culture + reflection

Use the final full day to connect monumental Rome with the smaller details of food, religion, neighborhoods, and daily routines.

Experience a final mix of major cultural sites and ordinary city life, then compare what feels different from your first day abroad.

You’re practicingSelf-awarenessReflection

What do you notice now that you would have missed on Day 1?

Choose the three moments that best explain how your understanding changed across the trip.
