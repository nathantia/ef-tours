Day 1 · Overnight flight

Cross the Pacific and reset your frame of reference.

Travel + anticipation

The learning starts before arrival: students prepare to enter a place where familiar routines, language, scale, and social expectations will work differently.

Travel overnight, review arrival routines, and set one personal intention for the experience ahead.

Day 2 · Tokyo

Arrive in a city of contrasts.

Arrival + orientation

Meet your Tour Director and begin reading Tokyo as a place where dense modern systems coexist with longstanding customs and rituals.

Get oriented, learn group rhythms, navigate the neighborhood, and experience your first meal and street-level impressions.

You’re practicingConfidenceCultural awareness

What feels most different first: pace, etiquette, transit, sound, signage, or personal space?

Capture one ordinary detail that immediately tells you you are somewhere new.

Class connection · Writing · Culture
Day 3 · Tokyo

Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara: old systems, new systems.

History + technology

Move from temple traditions and museum collections into Akihabara’s technology and pop-culture landscape.

Visit Asakusa Kannon Temple, explore Ueno, and compare historic ritual with contemporary consumer and technology culture.

You’re practicingOpen-mindednessObservation

Where does tradition remain visible inside one of the world’s most technologically advanced cities?

Choose two images—one historic, one contemporary—and explain what they reveal about continuity and change.

Class connection · History · Technology · Culture
Day 4 · Tokyo

Meiji, Harajuku & Shibuya: identity in public.

Culture + identity

See how sacred space, youth fashion, retail, and one of the world’s busiest crossings coexist within a short distance.

Visit Meiji Shrine, walk Harajuku, explore Shibuya, and navigate a supervised small-group experience.

You’re practicingAgencyCommunication

How do people signal identity through clothing, behavior, ritual, and the use of shared space?

Compare one unwritten social rule you notice in Tokyo with one at home.

Class connection · Sociology · Culture · Design
Day 5 · Kamakura • Hakone

Move from megacity to mountain landscape.

Belief + environment

Travel through Kamakura toward Hakone and see how religious sites, landscape, and regional identity change the rhythm of the trip.

Visit the Great Buddha and Hachimangu Shrine, then continue toward the Fuji-Hakone-Izu region.

You’re practicingCuriosityCultural sensitivity

How does the experience of a sacred site change when landscape and movement are part of the setting?

Create a short photo essay connecting place, belief, and scale.

Class connection · World religions · Geography
Day 6 · Hakone • Kyoto

See Fuji, then ride the system that connects Japan.

Nature + infrastructure

Experience Hakone’s landscape before boarding the shinkansen to Kyoto—moving from natural systems to one of Japan’s most recognizable transportation systems.

Explore Hakone, see Mount Fuji when weather permits, stop near Odawara, and travel by bullet train to Kyoto.

You’re practicingAdaptabilitySystems thinking

What does the bullet train reveal about how infrastructure can shape daily life and national identity?

Compare one transportation system in Japan with one at home: speed, access, behavior, and design.

Class connection · Geography · Engineering · Design
Day 7 · Kyoto

Kyoto: history is still part of the present.

History + design

Explore places shaped by imperial, shogunate, Buddhist, and craft traditions—and notice how preservation and everyday life overlap.

Visit the Golden Pavilion, Nijō Castle, and Arashiyama with expert context.

You’re practicingCultural awarenessPerspective

What choices make a place feel preserved rather than frozen in time?

Choose one architectural detail and explain what it communicates about power, belief, or aesthetics.

Class connection · History · Art · Design
Day 8 · Kyoto • Nara

Fushimi Inari & Nara: ritual, repetition, and memory.

Belief + continuity

Walk through torii gates, visit Nara, and study how religious spaces remain active parts of cultural life.

Visit Fushimi Inari-taisha, tour Nara, and see Todaiji Temple.

You’re practicingEmpathyCultural sensitivity

How does participating respectfully in a living religious space differ from visiting a monument or museum?

Write three guidelines for learning in a sacred place without turning it into a backdrop.

Class connection · World religions · Ethics · Culture
Day 9 · Hiroshima • Miyajima

History, memory, and responsibility.

History + reflection

Travel to Hiroshima and encounter how a city remembers catastrophe while also presenting a living, contemporary identity.

Visit Peace Memorial Park and Museum, then travel to Miyajima and Itsukushima Shrine.

You’re practicingEmpathySelf-awareness

What is the difference between learning a historical event and standing in a place built around remembering it?

Record a private reflection before discussing the experience with the group.

Class connection · History · Ethics · Civics
Day 10 · Himeji • Kansai

Read power through architecture.

Architecture + comparison

Visit Himeji Castle and use the final full day to compare how architecture, defense, ceremony, and public memory changed across the trip.

Travel via Himeji and explore one of Japan’s most significant surviving castles before returning to the Kansai region.

You’re practicingObservationSynthesis

Which place on the trip most changed your understanding of Japanese history—and why?

Build a three-image comparison connecting Tokyo, Kyoto, and Himeji.

Class connection · History · Architecture · Visual literacy
Day 11 · Return home

Return with more than photos.

Reflect + apply

The trip ends, but the learning keeps moving. Use the flight home—and the first days back—to decide what changed, what is useful, and what deserves to be shared.

Travel home with a collection of moments, conversations, questions, and observations that are now yours to interpret.

You’re practicingReflectionAgency

What do you see differently now that felt normal before the trip? What part of Japan keeps resurfacing in your thinking?

Choose one idea from the trip to apply in class, share with family, or pass to the next group of travelers.
